# Apple drag-test boundary

The final DaBin change retains its existing native NSURL and lazy encoded-image representations, backed by stable outgoing snapshots. The experimental NSFilePromiseProvider composition was omitted: its filename behavior had an observed gap, and the private-pasteboard fixture did not establish a valid accepted-drag transport test. No file-promise interoperability is claimed for the final change.

Apple documents a drag lifecycle: NSFilePromiseReceiver reads from the dragging pasteboard, and receivePromisedFiles is called when accepting the promise. The provider's write callback occurs after the drag completes. NSPasteboard.writeObjects only guarantees successful addition of items; it does not document that an arbitrary private pasteboard without a drag session must trigger file-promise fulfillment. Thus zero callbacks in that fixture, including with an original provider, cannot prove a defect in an actual accepted drag. It also cannot prove success. [Receiver](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsfilepromisereceiver), [acceptance and fulfillment](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsfilepromisereceiver/receivepromisedfiles(atdestination:options:operationqueue:reader:)), [provider callback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsfilepromiseproviderdelegate/filepromiseprovider(_:writepromiseto:completionhandler:)), [writeObjects](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nspasteboard/writeobjects(_:)).

Apple's official table-view sample subclasses NSFilePromiseProvider, preserves superclass promise handling, and adds file URL and custom row representations. It demonstrates a supported extension pattern, rather than proving equivalent behavior for arbitrary composition wrappers. Lazy image bytes marked NSPasteboard.WritingOptions.promised are a separate pasteboard-data promise, not proof of destination file-promise fulfillment. [Official sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/supporting-table-view-drag-and-drop-through-file-promises), [lazy data option](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nspasteboard/writingoptions/promised).

The corrected scope tests deferred URL and encoded-image reads against the snapshot fix. Experimental failures remain diagnostic evidence; they are not relabeled as passing transport tests. A future file-promise feature needs a real accepted drag, actual provider and receiver callbacks, and destination-byte verification before claiming interoperability. Direct delegate invocation verifies copying only.
